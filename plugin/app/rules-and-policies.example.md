# Rules & policies

The standing rules the routine follows. Yours to edit — the agent folds your answers in
here as you give them, and reads this file before every sweep.

This is a **starting template**, not a finished policy. It carries the *shapes* that have
proven useful; the specifics are yours to fill in.

---

## 1. One-time codes → always delete

Verification and 2FA codes expire in minutes and have no lasting value. They are moved to
Trash the moment they are read, and journalled like everything else.

## 2. Bills and receipts → file by sender

- A **receipt** confirms a completed payment → `receipts/<sender>/`
- A **bill** asks for money or review (statements, due notices, verifications) →
  `bills/<sender>/`
- Filename: `<mailbox-prefix>-<what-it-is>-<YYYY-MM-DD>.eml`, with a full ISO date.
- **Look in the sender folder before writing.** The same message filed twice under two
  invented names is the commonest way this tree rots.
- Paid → mention in the report. Needs action → top of the report.

## 3. Junk and marketing → Trash, journalled

Never permanently deleted. Trash is recoverable for about 30 days, and every removal is
written to the deletion journal with its reason.

## 4. Unsure → do not delete

Add it to your questions file and leave the mail alone until it is answered. One held
question costs a day; one wrong deletion can cost something irreplaceable.

## 5. Which mailbox is for what

_Fill this in._ The routine uses it to notice when mail turns up somewhere it does not
belong — a work message in a gaming mailbox is a finding, not a coincidence.

| Mailbox | Used for |
|---|---|
| _your primary_ | real-name, professional |
| _your alias_ | signups that do not need your legal name |

**Identity separation:** expose the minimum real information a service actually requires.
Do not volunteer a real name, address or phone where they are optional.

## 6. Always-keep senders

_Fill this in._ Banks, your employer, government, medical, legal, anyone whose mail must
never be missed. Anything from these is never junk, whatever it looks like.

Mirror these into `config/protected.local.json` — that file is what actually stops an
automatic rule from touching them.

## 7. Family → always escalate

_Fill this in._ Names, and how you want to hear about them.

**Judge on substance, not on who a post belongs to.** Trouble — illness, an accident, money
or housing, anything about children, a plea for help — escalates even when it arrives as a
comment on a stranger's thread. Ordinary chatter is a one-line mention. Bias toward
surfacing: a line you skim costs two seconds; a missed emergency is the failure this whole
routine exists to prevent.

Where a platform withholds the text (many notifications carry none), **say so plainly**
rather than implying it was read and cleared.

## 8. Junk threshold → start careful, ratchet up

Begin conservative. A borderline sender is listed as a *junk candidate* in the report, not
auto-trashed. Once you confirm one, it joins the confirmed-junk table below and is
auto-trashed from then on.

The dashboard can write that rule for you from the sender panel, but only on evidence: the
sender must have been binned every time, over enough messages, and must not touch anything
in your protected list.

## 10. Social and platform mail

Surface **action items** (security alerts, account notices) and **people reaching out**.
Everything else from these platforms — digests, job alerts, engagement bait — is junk.

## 11. Security alerts caused by this tool → delete; others → surface

Alerts generated by your own mail tooling are noise. Any alert that predates it, or that
does not match its footprint, is surfaced instead.

## 12. Summary-tier senders → digest, then retire

Newsletters worth a one-line summary but not worth keeping. Each gets a shelf life; past it,
the original is trashed.

**The shelf-life check runs against the WHOLE mailbox, never the fetch window.** A retention
question is by definition about older mail, so a check scoped to the last two days can only
ever see items too young to retire — and it will answer confidently anyway.

## 13. Be time-aware

Compare against the date the *event* happens, not when the mail arrived. A past-dated
appointment, deadline or offer is not actionable and does not belong in "needs your
attention".

## 14. Workflow mail → its own panel, and a push

_Fill this in if it applies to you._ Senders whose mail carries something you must **do**,
on a deadline, with a link — a clinic questionnaire, a video appointment, a portal notice.

Configure them in `config/protected.local.json` under `workflow_senders`. Their links are
made clickable **only** when the sender is verified by DKIM for your configured domain
*and* the destination is under that same domain. Both, or it is shown as text with the
reason.
